Why this plan is built this way ▸
  • Real hour budgets, not abstract "load" — 5-7h on weekdays, 8-12h on weekends, on top of coaching. Every task carries a realistic time estimate (a Physics/Chem sub-topic ≈1h, a whole Bio chapter ≈2.5h, a 20-question chunk ≈30min), so the day's plan is sized in actual hours you'll spend, not a vague unit.
  • All 86 chapters' first-time learning finishes by 1 Dec 2026 — every chapter, 11th and 12th, across all four subjects, is pulled forward to be learned by then regardless of when its own test would otherwise have allowed it. The 200-questions-per-chapter practice keeps running afterward via the daily question block, finishing naturally by around mid-to-late December — well before any AIATS actually needs it.
  • Class 11 : Class 12 in a 40:60 split — whatever time is available for new-topic learning each day is divided this way, with either side able to use the other's leftover room rather than waste capacity.
  • Organic Chemistry, sequenced properly — GOC, Hydrocarbons and Haloalkanes & Haloarenes are prioritised first and must be genuinely finished before Alcohols/Phenols/Ethers, Aldehydes/Ketones/Carboxylic Acids, Amines or Biomolecules get pulled ahead of schedule — matching how the topics actually build on each other.
  • A reserved daily question-solving slot — 1.5h on weekdays, 2h on weekends, ring-fenced so practice doesn't get crowded out by cramming new content.
  • Backward planning from test dates — Practice Tests, Term Exams & NRTs: topics finish 4 days before the test, then a self mock-test slot before you sit it. AIATS: everything is fully re-revised at least 10 days out, with 2 mock tests built in.
  • Bio revision, on a cycle — once a Botany/Zoology chapter is marked done, it quietly resurfaces roughly every ~25 days as optional filler to help keep Biology accuracy high through NEET.
  • Spaced repetition — once you check a topic off, light "quick revise" reminders resurface it at 1, 3, 7, 16 and 35 days later (the classic Ebbinghaus forgetting-curve spacing), rather than reading something once and never touching it again.
  • Active recall over re-reading — mock tests and per-chapter question practice are built into the plan itself, because testing yourself sticks better than re-reading notes.
  • Interleaving — each day mixes subjects rather than one giant single-subject block, which research on interleaved practice shows improves retention over "blocked" study.
  • A daily wind-down block — ~20-30 min for your web novel, every day, deliberately kept outside the study-hour count so it never feels like it's eating into your plan.
  • Backlog tab — anything whose due date slips by unchecked shows up there so it's never silently lost, even though the daily plan already rolls it forward automatically.
